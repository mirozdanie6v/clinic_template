# Commercial Proposal & Discovery Playbook

## Purpose

Use this playbook before creating a product blueprint for a client Mini App.
The outcome is not a generic "beautiful app" but a grounded decision model:
what a person is trying to achieve, what prevents the decision, which proof is
credible, and what the product must do next.

## Evidence discipline

- Separate **client-provided fact**, **publicly sourced fact**, **inference**
  and **open question**. Do not turn an inference into a claim.
- Cite a source URL beside every public fact that changes a UX, offer, CTA or
  trust decision. Record the date of access when it matters.
- Do not copy a competitor's text, branding, layout or unsupported claim.
- If web search is unavailable, mark the research gate `BLOCKED` and create a
  clearly labelled research plan; do not simulate research.

## Required discovery outputs

### 1. Business and offer model

Record target segment, geography, moment of need, main offer, conversion event,
sales/fulfilment path, operating limits, mandatory legal/medical boundaries,
existing proof and source reliability.

### 2. Decision psychology map

For every priority job-to-be-done capture:

| Field | Required question |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | What event makes the person look now? |
| Desired outcome | What progress or relief do they seek? |
| Anxiety/risk | What could make the choice feel unsafe or regrettable? |
| Objection | What would stop the next step? |
| Information need | What must be understood before acting? |
| Trust proof | What evidence is credible for this person? |
| Decision criterion | How will alternatives be compared? |
| Friction | What makes the action hard on mobile or in context? |
| Micro-commitment | What smallest honest next step is appropriate? |

### 3. Competitor and pattern matrix

Research and cite at least:

- three local/direct competitors;
- three international category leaders;
- two adjacent-category products that solve a similar decision problem;
- three visual/interaction references. Behance may be used here for visual
  patterns, but never as proof of business performance or implementation.

For each reference record: source, relevant user task, observed pattern,
business rationale, accessibility/mobile risk and one decision: `ADOPT`,
`ADAPT` or `REJECT`. The goal is informed differentiation, never cloning.

### 4. Proposal-to-product translation

Turn research into: priority user journeys, message hierarchy, proof inventory,
offer architecture, CTA ladder, content/data requirements, features to omit,
and measurable prototype acceptance criteria.

## Quality questions before handoff to UX/UI

- Can each primary CTA be traced to a specific user trigger and objection?
- Is proof factual, obtainable and located before the decision point?
- Does the mobile flow reduce cognitive load rather than merely compress desktop?
- Does the scope respect the actual operations of the business?
- Are all missing facts still visible as assumptions or open questions?

If any answer is no, do not issue a `PASS` Research & Discovery gate.
